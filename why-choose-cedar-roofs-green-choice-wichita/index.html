Why Choose Cedar Roofs as a Green Choice: Are Cedar Roofs Sustainable in Wichita?

By Rhoden RoofingUpdated Apr 23, 20265 min read
Roof TypesWood RoofsWichita

Let’s talk about something that actually matters when you’re investing in a roof—not just what it costs today, but what it does for your home, your energy bill, and yep, the planet. If you’re a homeowner in Wichita trying to make environmentally smart decisions, you’ve probably asked yourself, “Are cedar roofs sustainable?” or maybe even Googled “why choose cedar roofs as a green choice?”

At Rhoden Roofing LLC, we’re not just here to sell you shingles. We’re here to help you make educated decisions that align with your values, goals, and lifestyle. Whether you’re eco-conscious, budget-minded, or just want something that lasts and looks amazing—we’re breaking it all down for you.

Ready to dig into the real talk about cedar roofing and why it may be one of the smartest, greenest decisions you can make for your home? Let’s go.

The Challenge: Making Sustainable Choices Without Sacrificing Quality

As a homeowner, you want a roof that lasts, protects, and looks good—but you also care about the impact your choices have on the environment. The problem? Most roofing materials don’t check all those boxes. Asphalt shingles, for example, dominate the market, but they’re petroleum-based, short-lived, and often end up in landfills.

So what’s the solution? Cedar. But don’t take that at face value—let’s break down exactly why choosing cedar roofing is a smart move for your home and for the planet.

Cedar Roofs and Sustainability: Are Cedar Roofs Really a Green Choice?

Absolutely—when you choose high-quality cedar from responsible sources and install it the right way, you’re getting one of the most eco-friendly roofing options out there.

1. Cedar Is a Renewable Resource

Unlike metal, synthetic, or asphalt roofing, cedar comes from trees—renewable, natural, and biodegradable. Most cedar shingles are harvested from managed forests where replanting is part of the long-term plan. When a cedar tree is cut down, several more are planted in its place. That means you’re not just taking—you’re participating in a cycle of regeneration.

Bonus: At Rhoden Roofing, we source cedar from suppliers who follow sustainable forestry practices. You can rest easy knowing your beautiful roof didn’t come at the cost of a healthy ecosystem.

2. Low Carbon Footprint in Production

Producing cedar shingles uses far less energy compared to manufacturing metal panels or asphalt shingles. That means fewer greenhouse gas emissions released into the atmosphere.

No heavy chemical refining. No oil-based processes. Just saws, drying, and some optional natural treatment. It’s about as low-impact as roofing gets.

3. Biodegradability: It Goes Back to Nature

At the end of its lifespan, your cedar roof won’t sit in a landfill for the next 500 years. Cedar is completely biodegradable, unlike synthetic shingles, which contribute millions of tons of waste annually.

When your cedar shingles are done protecting your home (decades later), they decompose naturally—no toxic chemicals, no environmental harm.

Energy Efficiency: Cedar as a Natural Insulator

This is where cedar does some heavy lifting behind the scenes.

1. Keeps Your Home Cool in Summer and Warm in Winter

Cedar is a natural insulator. Its cellular structure creates an air buffer that helps regulate indoor temperatures more effectively than asphalt or metal roofing. That means less reliance on heating and cooling systems—lower energy bills, smaller carbon footprint.

In Wichita, where summers can be scorching and winters can dip below freezing, this is a game-changer.

2. Helps You Save Money Long-Term

Energy-efficient homes don’t just feel better to live in—they also cost less to run. Investing in cedar may come with a higher upfront cost, but the long-term savings in reduced energy use make it a smart financial move.

Rhoden Roofing can show you real-world examples of how cedar roofing has helped homeowners cut their utility bills and stay more comfortable year-round.

Durability + Longevity = Less Waste

A cedar roof, when installed by pros (hint: that’s us), can last 30 to 50 years with proper care. Let’s compare that to asphalt shingles, which usually need replacement every 15 to 20 years.

Fewer replacements mean fewer resources used, less waste, and more time between roofing projects. It’s the definition of sustainability: do it once, do it right, and don’t do it again for decades.

Need help with maintenance? Rhoden Roofing offers routine cedar roof inspections and tune-ups so your investment lasts as long as it should.

Common Questions from Wichita Homeowners

Let’s answer what we know you’re already thinking.

Isn’t a Cedar Roof More Expensive?

Yes, initially—but you’re getting serious value for your dollar. Between energy savings, increased home value, and reduced replacement costs, cedar often ends up being less expensive over the life of the roof.

Plus, you’re paying for aesthetics. Let’s be honest—a cedar roof makes your home look stunning.

How Does Cedar Hold Up in Wichita Weather?

Kansas weather can be extreme. Cedar can handle it. It’s naturally moisture-resistant, handles wind well, and we can treat it for added fire resistance. With proper installation and ventilation (our specialty), you’ve got a roof that works with Wichita’s seasons—not against them.

What About Fire Safety?

Today’s cedar shingles can be treated with fire-retardant solutions that meet Class B and even Class A fire ratings. Let us know your concerns, and we’ll walk you through your options.
