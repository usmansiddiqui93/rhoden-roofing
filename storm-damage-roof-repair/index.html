Types of Storm Damage

Storms bring various dangerous components to the table. Many different types of storm damage may impact your roof and its ability to keep your home safe and structurally sound. Some of the most common forms of roof storm damage that affect Wichita residents include:

Hail damage

Although storms aren’t always accompanied by hail, when it does fall, the damage could be quite significant. With the size of hailstones varying from place to place – and growing at that – there’s no telling what the extent of the damage could be.

Depending on the type of roofing material, hail damage may cause splits or cracks that reduce the protective nature of your roofing. Water may be able to seep through and cause further damage to your home. Also, UV exposure to some of the underlying roofing components may reduce the lifespan of your roofing material.

Wind damage

Wind damage as a result of storms could have just as bad an impact as hurricane damage. There is the big possibility of lifted or damaged shingles, or in some cases completely missing shingles. Homeowners may not be aware of the surface damage immediately, so it’s best to get your local roofing experts up on the roof for an inspection.

How do I know if my roof has storm damage?
There are a number of signs that point to obvious storm damage on a roof. Some of these include missing or damaged shingles, falling debris, and in extreme cases, water leaking into the home. If you suspect damage, get a professional roofing service provider in for an inspection immediately to avoid any further damage and nip the current damage in the bud.
Is it legal for a roofing contractor to waive my deductible?

No, waiving insurance deductibles constitutes insurance fraud. 

Is storm damage covered by insurance?

This is determined by your specific insurance policy and company. Most insurance companies cover acts of God like hail or high winds. The only way to know whether your policy covers storm damage is to contact your agent and carefully read your policy’s terms. 

This will differ across insurance policies and companies, but by and large, most insurance companies do cover storm damage, whether caused by rain, snow, hail or wind. Be sure to read the terms and conditions carefully.

How can I reduce my homeowner's insurance premiums?

We wrote a guide that walks homeowners through insurance premium discounts a new roof may provide. Below are some key takeaways from the article. 

  • Location, age, and materials matter. Older homes with higher-risk features cost more to insure.
  • Claims history follows you. Past claims are logged in CLUE and impact rates even if you switch carriers.
  • Weather risk inflates premiums. In Kansas and other hail-prone states, weather elevates rates.
  • Your roof greatly affects your premiums. Newer, impact-resistant roofs can significantly lower your premiums.
  • Not all carriers offer the same discounts. Ask your agent about new roof discounts, impact-resistant shingle savings, and other options.
  • Inflation affects everyone. Rising material costs increase premiums irrespective of claims.
  • Always ask your agent directly. Your home and risk profile are unique: there’s no one-size-fits-all for home insurance.
How long does it take to reroof a house?

The average home reroofing can take anywhere between 1-3 days. This will of course be extended in cases with a much larger than average surface area.
