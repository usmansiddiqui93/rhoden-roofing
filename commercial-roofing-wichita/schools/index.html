The Right Roofing Decision Is Not Always Replacement

Preserve what is worth preserving.

When a roof has years of service life remaining, preventive maintenance helps preserve its remaining useful life and keep your district's replacement timeline intact.
Regular maintenance is the most cost-effective way to extend the life of a roof, which is why many manufacturing warranties require bi-annual inspections for warranty coverage. We document all inspection findings and maintenance items in a record that is accessible for your district.

WHEN REPLACEMENT ENTERS THE PLAN

Reducing a School’s Total Cost of Ownership

When it’s time to replace the roof, the goal of most districts is not simply to buy a new roof. It is to select a system that can stay in service longer, withstand the conditions that shorten roof life, and push the next major roofing expenditure as far into the future as possible.

Why FiberTite for Schools?

FiberTite roof systems are built around a KEE-based membrane with heavy reinforcement and a four-layer construction designed to resist punctures, UV exposure, chemicals, and ponding water. Its ASTM-verified Environmental Product Declaration uses a 40-year reference service life, giving districts a longer planning horizon when replacement is the responsible next step.

For a district, longer service life is not just a roofing benefit. Fewer replacement cycles mean fewer major capital events, less disruption, and a lower total cost of ownership over time.

Rhoden Roofing is an independent installer not affiliated with FiberTite nor any individual manufacturer. We consult on the best roof system for your district.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the best time of the year to replace a school's roof?

For most schools, summer break is the best time to replace a roof because it reduces conflict with classes, student traffic and any occupied spaces. However, roof work can be completed at other times, such as fall or spring break, so an actively leaking or failing roof should not simply be left until summer. 

How often should a school district inspect its roofs?

A school roof should be inspected at least twice a year, typically in the spring and fall, and after major storms. Regular inspections help identify vulnerabilities before they cause interior leaks or damage. For schools, maintaining consistent inspection records also gives districts better information for maintenance and long-term capital planning. 

What determines whether a school roof can be repaired or needs replacement?

A school roof can often be repaired when the damage is isolated and the surrounding roof system, insulation, and deck remain dry and serviceable. Replacement, or a layover, is necessary when leaks are recurring, moisture has spread beneath the membrane, or continued repairs are simply more costly than a full replacement. 

The decision to replace a roof is not determined by its age alone, but by its documented condition and the district’s capacity to replace the roof. 

Can a school district replace its roofs in phases?

Yes. Large campuses often phase roof replacements by building or by roof section across multiple summers or budget cycles. High priority, vulnerable roof sections are replaced first, while other roof sections are maintained in the meantime. Rhoden Roofing has completed a K-8 roof replacement in three phases over three summers to spread both the capital expense and disruption. Consistent condition records make it easier to establish which roofs are the priority and update priorities over time. 

Why does Rhoden Roofing often recommend FiberTite instead of TPO or PVC for schools?

A maintained FiberTite is capable of doubling the service life of a maintained TPO or PVC roof in Kansas and Oklahoma. Schools, which typically use bonds or budgeted capital expenditures to fund roofing projects, FiberTite’s longevity reduces a district’s total cost of ownership by up to 50 percent.

FiberTite is often installed at data centers and food-processing facilities where leaks would be debilitating. Similarly, for schools, leaks require funding to repair — funding that is not always available, especially in the summer when HVAC repairs compete for capital dollars.

Our job is to help district leaders make the right decision for the district, whether it’s replacing a roof with FiberTite, TPO, or simply maintaining the roof a while longer.
