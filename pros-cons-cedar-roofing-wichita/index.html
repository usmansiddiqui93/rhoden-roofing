Pros and Cons of Cedar Roofing in Wichita

May 2, 20255 min readBy Rhoden Roofing
Roof TypesWood RoofsWichita

Let’s be real—choosing a roofing material isn’t something you do every day. So when it’s time to replace your roof, the pressure to get it right is real. You want a roof that’s beautiful, built to last, and makes financial sense in the long term. But you’re also smart—you’re doing your homework, and that’s why you’re here, reading up on cedar roofing.

Cedar roofs are known for their charm and longevity, but like any investment, they come with trade-offs. At Rhoden Roofing LLC, we believe in transparent advice, not marketing fluff. That’s why we’re diving into the benefits of cedar roofing, the disadvantages of cedar roofing, and what you really need to know if you’re considering cedar roofing in Wichita.

Because this isn’t just about roofing—it’s about making a confident decision that adds value to your home and fits your lifestyle.

Let’s get into it.

The Pros of Cedar Roofing

Cedar roofing isn’t just about looks (though let’s be honest—it’s a showstopper). It brings real performance advantages to the table.

1. Natural Beauty That Elevates Curb Appeal

There’s no other roofing material that gives you the same natural, warm, and timeless aesthetic as cedar. Whether it’s the rich amber hues of freshly cut shingles or the weathered silver-gray patina cedar takes on over time, this roof turns heads.

Why it matters:

  • Instantly elevates the architectural character of your home
  • Makes a strong first impression if you’re thinking about resale
  • Complements a range of home styles, from classic to contemporary

Translation: Cedar roofing isn’t just a roof—it’s a statement.

2. Built-In Insulation and Energy Efficiency

Here’s what most people don’t realize: Cedar is a natural insulator. That means it helps regulate your indoor temperatures year-round.

What this means for you:

  • Cooler indoor temps in the blazing Kansas summers
  • Less heat loss during freezing Wichita winters
  • Reduced demand on your HVAC system
  • Lower monthly energy bills

In a city like Wichita, where weather swings hard in both directions, this isn’t a small win—it’s a real financial benefit.

3. Long-Lasting Durability

When properly installed and maintained, a cedar roof can last 30 to 50 years. That’s a big jump from asphalt shingles, which usually tap out at around 20.

Why this matters:

  • Fewer replacements = lower long-term costs
  • Increased home value thanks to premium materials
  • A roof that stands up to Kansas wind, rain, and hail

Plus, cedar contains natural oils that make it resistant to rot, moisture, and insects. It’s like nature built it for resilience.

4. Environmentally Friendly

If you’re the kind of homeowner who cares about sustainability—and we think you are—cedar roofing is a smart choice.

Here’s the eco-friendly breakdown:

  • Cedar is a renewable resource
  • It’s biodegradable (unlike asphalt, which ends up in landfills)
  • It requires less energy to produce than synthetic roofing materials

If you’re looking for a roof that aligns with your values, this one checks a lot of green boxes.

The Cons of Cedar Roofing

We promised you straight talk, and that includes the downsides. Because even the best roofing material has its drawbacks. The key is knowing them up front so you can plan accordingly.

1. Upfront Cost Is Higher

There’s no getting around it—cedar roofing is more expensive than asphalt. Materials and skilled labor come at a premium.

What to consider:

  • It’s an investment, not a quick fix
  • Higher initial cost is offset by longevity and energy savings
  • It adds resale value, especially in upscale neighborhoods

Bottom line: If you’re looking for the cheapest roofing option, cedar isn’t it. But if you’re looking for long-term ROI, it should be on your shortlist.

2. Maintenance Is Required

Cedar isn’t a “set it and forget it” kind of roof. To get those 30 to 50 years of life, you’ve got to give it some TLC.

Typical maintenance tasks include:

  • Clearing off debris and leaves to prevent moisture buildup
  • Treating for moss, mildew, and insects
  • Reapplying sealants or preservatives every few years

It’s not a full-time job, but it does require some effort. The good news? Rhoden Roofing offers maintenance plans, inspections, and treatment services to make it easy on you.

3. Vulnerability to Fire Without Treatment

Let’s not sugarcoat it—untreated wood is flammable. That’s a concern for some homeowners, especially in areas prone to dry conditions or high winds.

But here’s what you should know:

  • Today’s cedar shingles can be treated with fire-retardant solutions
  • Many products meet Class A fire ratings when installed properly
  • Local building codes often require these treatments, and we make sure you’re covered

It’s not a deal-breaker—just a factor to consider. We’ll walk you through your fire safety options so you feel 100% confident.

4. Weathering and Aesthetic Changes

Cedar ages over time, and not everyone is into the natural gray patina it develops. Some love it. Others prefer to keep the fresh, amber look.

If you want to maintain its color, you’ll need to:

  • Apply stains or UV-protective coatings every few years
  • Keep it clean to reduce discoloration from debris or algae

This isn’t necessarily a con—it’s a style choice. But it’s worth understanding before you commit.

Is Cedar Roofing Right for Your Wichita Home?

That depends on what you’re looking for. If you want a budget roofing option with minimal maintenance, you might look elsewhere. But if you want a roof that delivers on beauty, performance, and long-term value, cedar is worth your serious consideration.

Wichita weather throws a lot at roofs—heat, cold, storms, wind. Cedar holds up impressively when installed by experienced professionals who understand the nuances of our local climate. That’s where Rhoden Roofing LLC comes in.
